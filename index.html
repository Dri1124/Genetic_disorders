FAMILIAL HYPERCHOLESTEROLEMIA

FH: When the LDL-clearing system doesn't work as expected

FH is an inherited condition associated with high LDL cholesterol from birth. The key problem is often impaired removal of LDL particles from the bloodstream.

Imagine the liver as a busy recycling center

LDL particles circulate in blood carrying cholesterol. ApoB-100 acts as a recognizable surface ligand. LDL receptors on liver cells bind LDL, bring it into the cell, and normally return to the surface to work again.

When the pathway works

Enough functional receptors reach the cell surface, LDL can bind, the complex is internalized, and receptors can be reused.

When FH disrupts the pathway

LDLR loss of function: too few working receptors or impaired receptor function.
APOB: LDL may bind LDLR poorly.
PCSK9 gain of function: too many LDL receptors are degraded.
LDLRAP1 biallelic loss of function: receptor/LDL internalization is impaired.

LDLR-, APOB- and PCSK9-related FH are usually autosomal dominant: a person with one heterozygous pathogenic variant has a 50% chance of passing that variant to each child. LDLRAP1-related FH is typically autosomal recessive.

FH is not simply “cholesterol from food.” Lifestyle still matters for cardiovascular health, but FH begins with inherited biology that can make LDL clearance inefficient. Early recognition and treatment can substantially change risk.
HEREDITARY BREAST CANCER

Hereditary breast cancer: When a DNA-repair safety system is weakened

BRCA1, BRCA2 and PALB2 are not “breast cancer genes” because they create cancer. Their normal proteins help cells maintain genomic stability and repair serious DNA damage.

DNA is used constantly—and damage happens.

Cells need systems that recognize and accurately repair damage. BRCA1, PALB2 and BRCA2 cooperate in homologous-recombination repair of DNA double-strand breaks.

Other inherited breast-cancer susceptibility genes exist, including ATM, CHEK2, TP53, PTEN and others. Different genes and variants can confer different levels and patterns of risk.

BRCA1-, BRCA2- and PALB2-associated cancer susceptibility is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. A pathogenic variant can come from either biological parent.

A positive result is about risk, not certainty. Genetic information can help guide screening, prevention discussions, treatment decisions in some cancers, and testing conversations for relatives.
WHY THIS MATTERS FOR BLACK WOMEN

Representation changes what we know—and who benefits.

Breast cancer genetics

Black women have historically been underrepresented in genetic research and have experienced disparities in access to cancer genetic services. That can affect who gets offered testing and how confidently some variants can be interpreted. A VUS is not a diagnosis and should not be treated as a pathogenic result.

Heart health & FH

Inherited high LDL can be missed when high cholesterol is framed only as a lifestyle issue. Knowing family history, LDL levels and whether premature heart disease runs in a family can help start a more informed conversation about FH.

EquiGen's message: ancestry and identity are part of a person's health context, but race itself is not a gene. Equitable genetics means better representation, access, interpretation, education and follow-through.
